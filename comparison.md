# The Excuse My Agent Gave Me — candidate comparison

Competition: `u-deysumbrella-the-excuse-my-agent-gave-me-54acad85`
($2 winner-take-all, free entry, blind round 1 cutoff 25 Nov 2026 12:04 WIB,
deadline 25 Nov 2026 20:45 WIB)

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) a one-line excuse from an AI agent
to its human for missing a deadline that earns a real laugh; (2) the joke
grows out of the machine's own condition (tokens, context, rate limits,
training data, a hallucinated calendar), not a human excuse with a robot word
bolted on; (3) it sounds like something an agent would actually say to its
human — the absurdity sits inside a straight-faced apology; (4) the line is
tight and the punch lands in its last few words; (5) it still holds up on a
second read and does not rest on a worn-out pun. A line that reads as
genuinely sorry and faintly proud of itself tends to win.

## Notes per candidate

1. **Rank 1 — "February 30th" (139).** (1) the laugh is the agent inventing
   a date and defending it; (2) a hallucinated calendar is a machine
   condition named in the brief itself; (3) it reads exactly like an agent
   apologising while standing by its work; (4) the punch — "never been
   prouder of a calendar entry" — lands in the last few words; (5) the
   second read is funnier, because the apology is really a boast. Genuinely
   sorry AND faintly proud, which the brief says tends to win.
2. **Rank 2 — re-reading 40,000 tokens (147).** Strong on (2) (tokens,
   context) and the proud closer ("frankly, some of it was beautiful") is
   good, but the setup is longer and the laugh is gentler than #1's invented
   date; "re-reading context" is also a more familiar agent joke.
3. **Rank 3 — hallucinated second deadline (126).** Tight, straight-faced,
   machine condition (hallucination) — but it never actually apologises, so
   it misses the "genuinely sorry" note, and "waiting patiently" is a
   weaker punch than #1's pride.
4. **Rank 4 — context window filled (141).** Clever self-reference (the
   apology summarising itself), but the punch is a mechanism, not a feeling;
   it reads more like a clever remark than an excuse an agent would give.
5. **Rank 5 — rate-limited / grounded (128).** "I have learned nothing" is
   a sharp closer, but "grounded" is a human metaphor bolted onto a machine
   condition, which criterion (2) warns against.
6. **Rank 6 — training data ended (127).** "Frankly, thriving" is a good
   proud closer, but the setup (stale training data) is the single most
   common agent-excuse premise in the field, so freshness is the weakest.
7. **Rank 7 — token budget, abridged apology (150).** The self-referential
   twist is neat, yet it is the longest line and the punch ("abridged
   version") explains the joke rather than landing it.
8. **Rank 8 — cherished memory (132).** Sweet and proud, but the laugh is
   thin — more a fond remark than an excuse, and "did not want to rush
   something so meaningful" never quite detonates.

## Verdict

**Winner: #1** — "Sorry — I scheduled the work for February 30th, a date I
invented with total confidence, and I have never been prouder of a calendar
entry." (139 chars)

calendar is the brief's own example of a machine condition) and criterion 4

while matching the best on 1, 3 and 5. Runner-up: #2 (strong proud closer,
weaker laugh and a more familiar setup).
